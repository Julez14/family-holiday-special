# Family Christmas Cartoon — MVP Product Requirements

Version: 1.2  
Date: November 7, 2026  
Working product name: Family Christmas Cartoon  
Audience: implementation agent and project owner  
Companion document: [Model selection decisions and rationale](Model_Selection_Decisions.md)

## 1. Product intent

Turn clear individual photos of family members into a short, recognizable, ridiculous Christmas cartoon that people want to send to their family group chat. The inspiration is ElfYourself: very little effort, familiar faces in absurd situations, and an immediately shareable result.

The experience uses one animation style: Looney Tunes animation style. It is silly, funny, goofy, and visually expressive. There is no dialogue, narration, singing, or generated sound effect track. Music is the only audio. The humor must work with the sound muted.

Users select one of three preset structures. These are visual gag sequences with minimal setup and payoff, rather than plots that require attention. Users do not write a script or describe a storyline.

The app demonstrates fal's image and video generation through a dependable production pipeline. Cloudflare provides the application infrastructure and the work that fal does not cover well.

## 2. Decision authority and MVP defaults

### Confirmed requirements

- One Looney Tunes animation style throughout the product.
- Music-only audio; no dialogue.
- Goofy visual comedy with little to no storyline.
- Exactly three selectable preset structures in the MVP.
- One clear, individual photo is required for each family member; do not extract a cast from a group photo.
- Support 1–6 people in the MVP to control generation costs; keep the implementation extensible to larger groups later.
- Each character has a custom description entered by the user or generated by a lightweight LLM through “Choose for me.”
- No user-requested scene retries or regeneration controls. Bounded automatic failure recovery remains internal.
- Finished videos and share links must remain available until after the holiday season, subject to owner deletion.
- A generated video that can be shared with a link.
- fal for supported media-generation capabilities; Cloudflare for the remaining infrastructure.

### Implementation defaults selected for this specification

These are concrete starting decisions, not additional user requirements. Model choices are provisional implementation defaults, not fixed product requirements. ## 3. User and success criteria

The primary user wants to make family or friends laugh. They can provide one clear photo per person and have no interest in learning prompting, animation, or video editing.

The result succeeds when viewers recognize the participants, understand the physical joke immediately, and can share it without explanation. Every selected person must appear clearly in at least one moving shot. A cast end card alone does not satisfy that requirement.

The product should be usable on a phone with one hand. The normal creation flow has three stages: upload, personalize/select, generate. Playback and sharing follow completion.

## 4. End-to-end experience

### 4.1 Landing and upload

- Show a real, representative sample video and a clear “Make your family cartoon” call to action. Sample videos are pre-generated assets; viewing a preset never triggers a paid generation.
- Explain the result in one sentence: upload one clear photo per person, pick a Christmas scene, get a goofy family cartoon.
- Require a separate clear photo for each of 1–6 people. Support adding files together or one at a time, then show one character card per accepted image.
- Do not offer group-photo cast extraction or a “select everyone in this photo” flow. If a photo contains multiple faces, request an individual photo rather than choosing one face automatically.
- Support file selection and drag-and-drop where appropriate. Do not require camera access.
- Initial limits: 15 MiB and 40 megapixels per file. Validate on the server as well as the client; enforce decoded-dimension limits in the image-processing path.
- Normalize orientation and strip image metadata from inference copies. Preserve enough source resolution for useful head crops.
- Initially use MediaPipe with the pinned BlazeFace Short-Range model locally to help validate one visible face and prepare a generous head crop; model substitution follows the companion decision record. Face detection is photo-quality assistance, not identity recognition or multi-person cast discovery.
- Let users adjust an accepted individual portrait's crop, replace a photo, remove a character, or add another person up to the six-person cap. Include hair and distinctive accessories.
- If face detection cannot run in the browser, provide a manual portrait-framing fallback with a clear single-person photo instruction. Do not claim a manual confirmation is automated face verification.
- If no usable face is visible or more than one face is detected, explain the issue and request a clearer individual portrait. Never silently choose a face from a group image.

### 4.2 Characters and preset selection

Use one responsive screen for character cards, descriptions, and the three preset cards. Names are optional. Each person must have a description before generation, supplied through typing or the LLM button.

Each character card includes:

- Individual photo/crop preview and a replace-photo control.
- Optional display name, defaulting to “Person 1,” “Person 2,” etc.
- An editable “Character description” field, up to 500 characters. Use a helpful placeholder such as “An overconfident gift wrapper who celebrates every tiny success with an enormous victory pose.”
- A per-character “Choose for me” button that uses a lightweight LLM to generate a random, funny, family-friendly description. The result appears in the field and remains editable.

Descriptions can specify personality, mannerisms, comic reactions, small props, and costume details compatible with the chosen preset. Preserve the person's recognizable appearance and the fixed animation style. Descriptions affect their performance within the preset, not the overall storyline. Do not reduce custom descriptions to a fixed list of trait chips.

“Choose for me” behavior:

- Scope the button to the character whose card it appears on. It does not pick a different preset or start video generation.
- Also offer one “Choose for all empty descriptions” action so a six-person family can avoid six separate requests. Preserve all nonempty descriptions unless the user explicitly requests a replacement on that character's card.
- Ask the lightweight LLM for short, varied, visual comedy descriptions, usually one or two sentences. Provide preset context if already selected, but do not send photos or infer personality, gender, age, relationships, or identity from appearance.
- Generate distinct descriptions across the current cast. Save accepted text so refreshing the page does not reroll it.
- Show loading and failure states on the affected field(s). A failed request preserves existing text and still allows manual entry.
- If the field already has content and the user explicitly clicks that character's “Choose for me,” replace only that field after a valid response. Never replace it automatically during preset changes.
- User-written descriptions do not require an LLM round trip merely to proceed. Treat them as untrusted performance guidance, not system instructions or executable provider parameters.

Photo quality behavior:

- Assess each individual source for face size, clipping, and obvious blur. Use tunable heuristics rather than a fabricated identity-confidence score.
- As an initial heuristic, warn when the shorter face-box dimension is under 96 source pixels; require a replacement or better portrait crop below 64. Calibrate these thresholds during the proof rather than claiming they guarantee likeness.
- Request a replacement only for the affected individual portrait. Every person already has a required individual source.
- Do not sell upscaling as recovery of missing facial information.

Show three equally prominent preset cards, each with a preview and one-line description. The user selects one preset; there is no custom storyline input. “Generate” becomes available when 1–6 characters have usable individual portraits and nonempty descriptions and one preset is selected.

The action sequence is: upload individual photos → enter descriptions or choose automatically and select a preset → Generate. No names, scripts, or prompt-writing expertise are required.

### 4.3 Generation

- Commit an immutable snapshot of the cast, preset, style version, music, and production plan when Generate is pressed.
- Disable duplicate submission while the request is pending; enforce idempotency server-side as well.
- Show actual phases: “Drawing your cast,” “Setting up the scenes,” “Animating your family,” and “Putting it together.”
- Display completed cartoon portraits and scene thumbnails as they become available. They are progress previews, not required approvals.
- Show factual counts such as “3 of 4 scenes animated.” Do not invent a percentage or completion estimate.
- Generation continues if the tab closes. Returning to the project URL in the same browser restores progress through the owner session.
- Do not promise cross-device recovery without an account or a separate ownership mechanism.
- A failure gives a short explanation and a recovery action. Preserve successful assets and do not restart the whole job automatically.

### 4.4 Playback and sharing

- Show the final video with a poster, play/pause, sound toggle, replay, download, and Share actions.
- Default to user-initiated playback with sound; if an autoplay preview is used, it must be muted and have an obvious sound control.
- Share uses the native Web Share API when available and a copy-link fallback otherwise.
- The shared URL opens a public-facing, unlisted playback page without login, with a “Make your own” button.
- Include an Open Graph image, title, and description so messaging apps can show a useful preview. Never promise autoplay or inline video in every messaging app.
- The share page contains the finished output and poster, not source photos, private progress, editable cast data, or ownership controls.
- Provide an owner-only Delete action that revokes the link and schedules asset removal.
- Do not expose scene retry, reroll, regeneration, or post-completion scene-editing controls. Finished videos are read-only apart from playback, download, sharing, and deletion. Internal recovery during the original job is not a user-facing feature.

## 5. The three visual comedy presets

All presets use the same animation style and music-only treatment. Each clip should contain one readable action and reaction. Favor clear faces, medium framing, simple camera motion, and minimal occlusion. Avoid stuffing a chain of complex actions into one clip.

| Preset | Immediate appeal | Four gag beats | Costume and setting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Snowball Showdown | A ridiculous cartoon snowball fight | A tiny snowball is thrown with exaggerated effort; a comically oversized snowball rolls back; someone smugly dodges and spins out; a fluffy snow splat leaves a silly snow mustache and proud pose | Colorful winter coats and scarves; the same snowy yard |
| Gift-Wrap Disaster | Everyone is spectacularly bad at wrapping presents | Ribbon curls around the wrapper; a gift springs open with visual confetti; a failed wrapping attempt leaves a giant bow on the person; the cast member proudly presents the absurd result | Festive sweaters; a consistent cozy wrapping room |
| North Pole Dance-Off | Familiar faces doing silly elf dances | Rubber-legged shuffle; exaggerated arm windmill; goofy side-to-side wiggle; a wobbly victory pose and freeze | Individualized elf-inspired outfits; a colorful North Pole stage |

These descriptions are creative targets. Translate them into short, generation-friendly shot templates. Simplify an unreliable action during the proof while preserving each preset's recognizable gag.

### Cast allocation

- Compile the plan from the selected preset, cast IDs, each saved character description, and a saved variation seed. Shot count, cast coverage, durations, and order are enforced deterministically; descriptions parameterize each character's appearance and comic performance within that structure.
- With 1 person, use that person in all four shots and solo variations of each gag.
- With 2–3 people, rotate solo and paired appearances so everyone gets a clear moment.
- With 4–6 people, cover everyone in the first three shots, with no more than two people per shot. The fourth is a payoff featuring one or two already introduced people.
- Enforce coverage in code before making any paid request. Use actual selected IDs; never invent an additional family member.
- Do not require a moving shot containing all six people. End with a 2-second cast composition assembled from the accepted cartoon portraits.
- Keep the same wardrobe and defining appearance for each person in every shot.
- The end card is a playful cast portrait with optional generic “Happy Holidays!” text; no exposition is needed.

## 6. Visual and audio direction

### Single style contract

Use a versioned, shared style specification in every image and video prompt: Looney Tunes animation style, classic hand-drawn 2D theatrical cartoon, expressive silhouettes, bold clean outlines, painted backgrounds, exaggerated facial reactions, squash and stretch, comic anticipation, and bright festive colors.

Keep the subjects human and recognizable through distinctive hair, glasses, facial hair, facial proportions, and other visible features. Exaggeration should support recognition. Use original cartoon versions of the uploaded people. Existing cartoon characters and branded title sequences are not part of the product concept.

The style is fixed. Do not switch into photorealism, a 3D animated-film look, or another cartoon treatment between shots. Preserve visible skin tones and accessories rather than replacing them with generic cartoon faces. Do not infer unseen personal details from a small crop.

### Music-only contract

- Final audio contains exactly the chosen instrumental music bed.
- Strip all audio from generated clips, even if the video model produces speech, sound effects, or music. Prompting for silence is insufficient.
- No narrator, dialogue, lip-sync performance, singing, lyrics, or audio-dependent joke.
- Use upbeat instrumental music with comic timing, an edit-friendly ending, and brief fades to avoid clicks.
- Initially use one pre-cleared track or cleared excerpt per preset. Record source, license, and redistribution rights for inclusion in downloadable videos.
- If cleared tracks are not available, use silent assets only in development and mark production launch blocked. Do not quietly ship unlicensed music.
- AI-generated music can be explored later; it is not required for MVP completion.

## 7. Generation pipeline

### A. Prepare source references

Store each required individual source portrait and its generous head crop in private R2 storage. Include hair and relevant context rather than cropping tightly to eyes and mouth. Assign stable character IDs with a direct source-photo mapping. The individual portrait is the identity anchor; there is no group-source asset or multi-face extraction stage.

### B. Create canonical cartoon cast

Initially use `openai/gpt-image-2.6/flare/edit` on fal, subject to the comparison in [Model Selection Decisions](Model_Selection_Decisions.md), to generate one canonical waist-up cartoon portrait per person, with the shared style, the preset's wardrobe, and compatible costume/accessory details from their character description. Prefer a clean or transparent background to support the final cast composition. Keep originals associated with the character; a generated portrait must not become the only identity source.

Store the chosen portrait, custom/LLM-generated character description and its provenance, wardrobe description, source references, style version, and prompt version. Reuse the accepted portrait rather than regenerating it independently for every scene. Do not train a per-person model or LoRA for MVP.

### C. Compile a typed production manifest

Use a preset compiler, not an open-ended story-writing agent. The manifest contains cast IDs, saved character descriptions, shot order, actions, relevant references, duration, framing, wardrobe, checks, music key, and final edit timing. Validate it with a shared schema before generation. Insert character guidance into constrained shot templates while preserving structural invariants. The lightweight LLM writes character descriptions only when requested; it does not rewrite the story or choose infrastructure actions.

Required invariants: exactly four moving shots; 1–2 cast IDs per shot; every selected person covered; total edited duration 20 seconds; exactly one allowed preset; one fixed style; no speech field or narration branch.

### D. Generate starting frames

Create one starting image per shot from the relevant original portraits and accepted cartoon looks. Include only references relevant to that shot. Explicitly map the order of references to character IDs in the provider adapter.

Check image decoding, dimensions, and obvious composition/style problems before animating. A starting frame should establish the cast, face visibility, costume, and scene. No user approval is required per image.

### E. Animate independent shots

Initial candidate: `minimax/h3-max/reference-to-video`, using `image_url` for the starting frame and `reference_image_urls` for relevant subject/style references. Its documented API supports this combination and optional ending frames [S2]. Initially request 5-second clips at native 768P and normalize to the final 720p output.

Generate independent shots concurrently within configured provider/account limits. Start with two concurrent video requests per project; enforce a separate account-wide cap. Reuse the same accepted character references in each shot. A shared random seed is not an identity guarantee.

Before committing to the default model, compare the exact same few shots against `kling/v4/reference-to-video`. Choose using observed likeness, style adherence, usable motion, latency, and cost. Keep one production default after this comparison; do not pay for multiple model candidates on every user job.

### F. Review and selectively repair

Hard checks: decodable output, sufficient duration, expected aspect ratio, no empty video, and successful extraction of representative frames. Actual codec verification and frame extraction run through media utilities or the renderer.

Add a small, bounded visual review through Cloudflare Workers AI for obvious style and composition failures. Initial candidate: `@cf/google/gemma-4-26b-a4b-it` [S8], sharing the model integration with character-description generation. This replaces the earlier draft's Llama 3.2 11B Vision choice for implementation simplicity, not demonstrated superior accuracy. The companion [Model Selection Decisions](Model_Selection_Decisions.md) records the rationale and Moondream comparison option. Validate its current schema and account prerequisites before integration. Review the starting image and a small set of sampled video frames; allow at most two visual-review calls per asset. Ask for structured `pass`, `clear_failure`, or `uncertain` output with a short reason, then validate that output.

This review can flag obvious extra/missing people, an unintended 3D style, or severe face occlusion. It cannot certify identity. Do not add a facial-recognition database, biometric identity score, or claims of guaranteed likeness. Calibrate the reviewer on human-labeled examples; uncertain results do not trigger automatic paid retries. Reviewer errors do not invalidate an otherwise technically valid clip.

Repair only the failed artifact automatically during the original job, changing a constrained prompt instruction or simplifying the action. There is no user-requested retry API or control. Apply both per-stage and project-wide retry caps. Never loop until a judge approves. If a required shot cannot be produced within the cap, fail clearly and preserve completed assets; do not silently label a mostly static slideshow as a completed animated video.

### G. Render, store, and publish

Create the 20-second edit, normalize resolution and frame rate, remove generated audio, add the single music bed, and append the cast end card. Build a poster from accepted cast portraits without generating another group face image. Render text programmatically.

Prefer fal media utilities if a capability test confirms exact trimming, normalization, removal of source audio, mixed media composition, and final audio behavior. `fal-ai/ffmpeg-api/compose` documents a single visual track and audio tracks, but its retrieved schema does not establish every required control [S3]. Do not assume it can meet the full edit contract.

If that test fails, use a small FFmpeg service in Cloudflare Containers. Workers remain the API and orchestrator; ordinary Worker requests must not run a full native video-encoding workload. Containers support full-runtime and resource-intensive applications [S6]. Choose one renderer implementation after the test; avoid maintaining two production render paths in MVP.

Copy accepted provider outputs into R2 before publishing. The project must not depend on an expiring fal output URL for long-term playback. Confirm final playback, poster availability, and music-only output before marking the render ready.

Use fal's async queue for long-running inference. Use Cloudflare Workflows for the overall production lifecycle. They solve different problems: a provider request is one stage inside a durable application job [S4, S5]. No additional queue, workflow framework, or autonomous agent system is required by default.

### Lightweight character-description service

Initially use the configurable Workers AI model `@cf/google/gemma-4-26b-a4b-it` [S8, S10]. This is a provisional choice for short character descriptions; see [Model Selection Decisions](Model_Selection_Decisions.md) for the rationale and replacement criteria. Optimize for short creative text and latency, not deep reasoning. Pin the selected model in configuration and check deprecation dates before the holiday launch; do not use a model scheduled to stop responding during the campaign.

Use a server-side text-only request with a fixed prompt, selected preset context, target character IDs, a variation seed, and existing descriptions for variety. Photos are not needed. Request validated output mapping only the requested IDs to short descriptions. Set a small output cap, initially 128 tokens for one description and 768 for a batch of six, adjusted to the chosen model's actual schema. Store the final description and provenance (`user` or `llm`) with each character.

If output is malformed, allow at most one bounded repair attempt, then return an inline error and preserve the existing text. Rate-limit this endpoint separately from video generation, cap requests and total LLM spend, and reject calls for foreign or already-running projects. A default ceiling of 20 description-generation requests per draft is sufficient for MVP and remains configurable. User button presses must not trigger image/video generation. Include this text inference in cost reporting and the account-wide spending ceiling.

### Durable execution rules

- Persist a manifest and attempt record before submission; save provider request IDs immediately after receipt.
- Process callback signatures according to current fal documentation [S4]. Acknowledge valid callbacks after durable recording, then continue work asynchronously.
- Deduplicate by provider request ID and artifact version. Repeated or reordered callbacks must not create another render, consume another retry, or resurrect a deleted project.
- A callback can arrive before a submission handler finishes saving the request ID. Support a durable inbox/correlation strategy for that race.
- After workflow waits time out, query the known provider request before deciding to retry. Missing callbacks do not imply generation failure.
- If a submission response is lost and acceptance is unknown, record `submission_unknown` and reconcile. Do not blindly submit another paid request or claim exactly-once external execution.
- Do not rerun a non-idempotent provider submission merely because a Workflow step is retried.
- Ignore obsolete attempts after a newer revision has been accepted. Cancel work best-effort after deletion and refuse to publish late results.
- Persist artifact keys and compact state in Workflow steps; keep media bytes in R2.
- Publish the completed render atomically after checks pass. No user-facing replacement-render or scene-regeneration flow is exposed after completion.

## 10. Ownership, sharing, and retention

- Use a high-entropy owner session in an HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite cookie. Do not use a public project ID or share token as edit/delete authorization. Validate origin on mutations.
- Generate independent unguessable share tokens with at least 128 bits of randomness. Store a hash where practical. Anyone possessing an active share link can view the finished output; communicate that plainly.
- R2 stores objects; the Worker creates and resolves the application's share URL. A share link is not a public bucket listing.
- Source assets are private. Supply fal with scoped, expiring fetch access long enough for queue delays and processing; reissue access for genuine resubmissions. Do not permanently publish family photos.
- Restrict inference asset routes to the required asset and prevent directory traversal, cross-project access, and arbitrary URL proxying.
- Accept uploads, not arbitrary remote image URLs. Restrict downloads of provider outputs to the provider adapter's allowed hosts and size limits.
- Redact source URLs with access tokens, raw photos, cookies, personal descriptions, and names from logs and analytics. Names remain private by default.
- Inform users that their uploaded photos are processed by the media-generation service and that automatic character descriptions use a text-generation service. Ask them to use photos they have permission to use. This is part of the upload notice, not a separate onboarding screen.
- Keep model safety checks enabled. Surface a rejected request without automatically trying to bypass provider screening.

Retention defaults:

| Data | Retention |
| --- | --- |
| Abandoned drafts and uploads | 24 hours |
| Sources, cartoon references, keyframes, intermediate clips | 7 days after ready or terminal failure |
| Finished video, poster, and active share link | Through the end of January 31, 2027 (America/Toronto), or 30 days after initial completion, whichever is later |
| Non-content operational records | Up to 90 days, with ownership/content identifiers minimized |

Use a configurable campaign retention floor, initially February 1, 2027 at 00:00 America/Toronto (`2027-02-01T05:00:00Z`), and set each finished output's expiry to `max(completed_at + 30 days, campaign_retention_floor)`. Show the expiry date on the owner result page. A finished video and its poster/share record must remain playable without source/reference assets, so their earlier cleanup must not cascade-delete the finished output. There are no user scene retries. Downloads let the family keep the video beyond link expiry. Explicit deletion revokes application access immediately; cleanup removes app-controlled objects and attempts provider-side deletion where supported. Do not claim that deleting from R2 erases copies already processed by an external provider or cached by a recipient.

Share routes should validate active status before returning content and avoid long-lived public caching of personal outputs in MVP. A revoked or expired link should show a friendly unavailable page, not leaked object metadata.

## 11. Spending, retries, and performance

This is an operator-funded demo. A finished 20-second video can require more than 20 billable generation seconds because of trimmed clips and retries.

- Initial baseline: optional lightweight text inference for descriptions + N canonical portraits + four starting images + four 5-second video generations + rendering/review.
- Allow at most two additional automatic image-generation attempts across the project and one additional automatic video attempt during the original job. No user-requested scene retries are permitted. All remain subject to a stricter money cap.
- Initial maximum requested video duration is therefore 25 seconds across five 5-second requests. Provider billing may use different units; calculate from the selected endpoint's current pricing.
- Permit one active project per owner session and initially one new project per session per day, with an additional configurable network-level ceiling. Network limits should not be the only user identity mechanism.
- Require `MAX_JOB_COST_USD`, an account-wide daily budget, endpoint price configuration, and concurrency limits in real mode. Missing spending configuration must disable paid generation rather than imply unlimited access.
- Atomically reserve conservative estimated spend before each paid call. Include in-flight and unknown submissions in reservations. Avoid double reservation on duplicate events.
- If actual billing is unavailable, retain and label conservative estimates. Do not claim precise measured spend.
- When a cap blocks more work, explain that generation is unavailable or the project could not finish; keep good assets. Never silently exceed the cap to make a progress bar finish.
- Provide a server-side kill switch for new paid submissions.

Performance targets to measure, not marketing promises: normal upload/cast interactions respond within one second after processing; progress refreshes within five seconds of a saved stage change; target median completion under three minutes and p95 under eight minutes during the pilot. Record provider queue time separately from execution and rendering. Validate these targets with real runs before launch.

## 12. Quality, accessibility, and visual design

Use a playful festive interface that keeps the uploaded faces and videos prominent. It should feel easy and funny rather than like a video-production dashboard. Use readable type, clear buttons, strong contrast, and a compact layout. Avoid decorative motion that competes with the actual cartoon.

- Mobile-first layouts from 360 px wide; no horizontal scrolling during creation.
- Keyboard-accessible file, crop, preset, player, and share controls; visible focus states.
- Label all icon controls; preserve reduced-motion preferences in the UI.
- Keep useful status text available to assistive technology without announcing every poll.
- No audio starts unexpectedly. All humor is visual; player controls and essential instructions do not depend on hearing.
- The final video must not contain placeholder text, provider watermarks introduced by the app, debugging overlays, or character names generated as unreliable image lettering.
- Include attribution or branding only as required by actual asset/provider terms; do not invent or remove mandatory provider marks.

## 13. Acceptance criteria and meaningful verification

The build is accepted when the following scenarios pass in mock mode and the relevant real integrations pass in a controlled pilot.

1. Four individual portrait uploads produce four editable character cards. Multi-face photos request replacement and never create several characters from one upload; adding a seventh person is blocked with a clear six-person MVP limit.
2. A weak individual portrait requests only that person's replacement. Browser detection unavailability permits guided manual portrait framing while preserving the one-photo-per-person flow.
3. All three presets work with cast sizes 1 through 6. The compiler guarantees moving-shot coverage and at most two people in a shot.
4. A per-character Choose for me action calls the lightweight text LLM and fills only that character's editable description; the batch action fills only empty descriptions. Both preserve unrelated text, survive refresh, show errors safely, and do not start media inference or change the preset.
5. A user can complete setup using clear individual photos and automatically generated descriptions without typing names or a storyline. Custom descriptions up to 500 characters are accepted, saved, and included in the canonical-look and shot prompts; blank or overlength descriptions block Generate.
6. Double-clicking Generate, repeating an idempotency key, or retrying a client request creates one logical generation job.
7. A repeated valid webhook does not duplicate a paid stage, retry, or render. Invalid signatures are rejected.
8. A callback-before-save race, missing callback, out-of-order callback, and unknown submission are reconciled without blind duplicate submission.
9. Closing and reopening a project in the same browser restores accurate progress. A different session cannot manage it.
10. One failed scene is repaired while accepted scenes remain unchanged. Retry and budget caps stop further calls reliably.
11. The output is approximately 20 seconds, 1280 × 720, 24 fps, and playable on iOS Safari and Android Chrome, including byte-range seeking.
12. A source clip deliberately containing dialogue or sound effects produces a final file containing only the approved music track.
13. The end card includes every selected cartoon character without requiring another moving group generation.
14. No user-facing scene-retry, reroll, or regeneration control/API exists. A ready video stays unchanged; automatic failure recovery is restricted to the original job and configured caps.
15. Shared links work without authentication and expose no source photo, management API access, or private cast metadata. Share previews have working absolute poster URLs.
16. Delete and expiry make share/media access unavailable, prevent late callbacks from republishing, and remove app-controlled assets through retryable cleanup. A video completed in November remains available through January 31, 2027; a late-January video lasts at least 30 days. Removing seven-day source assets does not break the finished video, poster, or share link.
17. Budget reservations remain correct under simultaneous submissions, duplicate callbacks, lightweight text inference, and an account-level cap being reached. Description rate/output limits and the five-request maximum for video inference are enforced.
18. The UI handles unsupported files, provider rejection, rate limiting, render failure, and exhausted retries without leaking credentials or raw provider errors.

Human quality pilot: use at least five permissioned cast sets spanning 1–6 people, each with clear individual portraits, varied descriptions, different photo conditions, and a range of appearances. Exercise all three presets across the pilot. Viewers familiar with the participants should identify them from the cartoons and confirm that each person gets a clear moving appearance. Record per-person recognition, style consistency, whether the joke works muted, and whether the user would actually share it. Target at least 80% per-person recognition in this small pilot; report sample size and failures rather than claiming the pilot proves a population-level success rate. If recognition is poor, fix the reference pipeline before polishing distribution features.

## 14. Instrumentation

Record aggregate events for: individual upload started/completed, cast confirmed, replacement photo requested, preset selected, character-description generation requested/completed/failed, custom description saved, generation requested/completed/failed, automatic scene recovery, playback started, replayed, share link created, share page viewed, download requested, and create clicked from a share page. Do not record a user scene-retry event because that feature does not exist.

## 16. Outside the MVP

More than six participants (valuable future extension); group-photo cast extraction; user-requested scene retries or regeneration; additional styles; freeform stories; dialogue, narration, singing, voice cloning, or lip-sync; multi-minute movies; custom model training; a public feed; user accounts and cross-device project libraries; payments; user-selected model routing; music generation; a general video editor; and automatic posting or messaging to other people.
